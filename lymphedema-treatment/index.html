What is lymphedema?

Lymphedema is swelling caused by a build-up of lymph fluid when the lymphatic system can’t drain it properly. It most often affects an arm or a leg.

Some people are born with differences in their lymphatic system. More often, lymphedema develops after the lymph nodes or vessels are damaged, for example by cancer surgery, radiation, infection or injury.

Common signs

  • Swelling in part or all of an arm or leg, including fingers or toes
  • A feeling of heaviness or tightness
  • Clothing, rings or shoes that feel tighter than usual
  • Less flexibility in the affected limb
  • Skin that becomes thicker or harder over time

Get urgent care for sudden swelling in one leg or arm, especially with pain, warmth or redness, or with shortness of breath. These can be signs of a blood clot or infection.

How we help

Dr. Cruz-Tolentino evaluates your swelling to find its cause and confirm the diagnosis. She then builds a personalized management plan to reduce symptoms, protect your skin and help you stay active, with regular follow-up.

Lymphedema and lipedema can occur together and are often confused. Getting the right diagnosis is the first step toward the right care.
